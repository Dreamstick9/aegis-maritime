# Decision log

Concise record of the choices made while building the prototype (chronological).

1. **Stack** — Vite 8 + React 19 + TypeScript 5.9, React Three Fiber/drei for the globe and vessel, Zustand for shared state, Recharts for charts, Vitest for tests, ESLint (typescript-eslint, react-hooks). Fonts: system humanist stack for UI; IBM Plex Mono bundled locally via `@fontsource` (OFL) for data only. No backend, keys or paid services.
2. **Coastlines** — Natural Earth (public domain) 1:110m globally plus 1:50m clipped to the operating region (`scripts/clip-natural-earth.mjs`, Sutherland–Hodgman clipping) so the routing land mask and the globe use real coastlines without a heavy bundle (≈185 KB total).
3. **Digital ocean** — analytic monsoon climatology plus seeded value noise instead of fetched Copernicus/ERA5 fields, so the demo is offline and reproducible; the README explains the production replacement.
4. **Routing** — A* on a 0.25° grid with 16-direction moves, land/coast buffer/under-keel/restricted-zone blocking, four cost weightings (shortest, weather, offshore, current) → K candidate corridors; fixed pilotage waypoints for the straits the grid cannot resolve; constraint-aware Douglas–Peucker simplification.
5. **Optimiser** — categorical Q-registers (a qudit-style generalisation of Q-bits) with observation and rotation-style updates toward Pareto guides for discrete choices; QPSO for the speed profile; crowding-pruned Pareto archive; Monte-Carlo hypervolume for convergence; CVaR over seeded weather perturbations (post-hoc in BALANCED, in-loop in ROBUST).
6. **Main-thread time slicing** — the optimiser is a generator driven by a MessageChannel scheduler (immune to background-tab timer throttling) with display pacing of ≥55 ms per generation so progress is legible; pure compute time is reported separately in the UI.
7. **Port draught realism** — the 128,000 t cargo on a Capesize gives ≈14.8 m sailing draught against Mormugao's 14.1 m inner channel; modelled as a *conditional* pass (tidal window or anchorage top-up) rather than hiding the conflict.
8. **Honesty labels** — every dynamic value carries a provenance mark (sourced / design inference / scenario input / synthetic); lifecycle factors are labelled illustrative; benchmarks are labelled synthetic and make no quantum-advantage claim.
9. **Visual world, first redesign** — the first pass was a card-and-badge dashboard; it was replaced with a globe-first composition ("The Bridge at Night": one contextual inspector, a layers drawer, a conning strip, hairline structure, a sea-glass accent). Kept: the composition. Discarded: the accent system and serif narrative.
9b. **Visual world, second redesign ("The Dark Cockpit")** — on the user's direction the world was replaced again: almost-black ink canvas, one safety-yellow signal reserved for the selected plan / primary action / active state, red only for computed alarms, warm off-white system-font typography at a 15px floor, thin rules and big blocks, mandatory progressive disclosure (panel default = name, four metrics, plan control, one sentence). Inspiration limited to the information hierarchy and restraint of industrial control products; no third-party branding or assets. Recorded in `DESIGN.md`, `PRODUCT.md` and `.impeccable/surfaces/`.
10. **Reduced motion** — respected via a store flag (initialised from `prefers-reduced-motion`): no auto-rotation, pulses or route fades; the globe renders on demand.
11. **Review loop (session 2)** — the redesign went through the Impeccable process: PRODUCT.md + DESIGN.md + a Command surface brief; a dual-agent critique (design review 26/40 with one P0, detector/browser evidence), a technical audit (12/20, six P1s) and a finish review. Fixes applied: active-plan lead numerals after a re-plan, paused auto re-plan, one primary action, brightness-plus-word warn semantics, scoped hotkeys, labelled control groups, 24px timeline hit areas, quantised strip updates, lazy-loaded routes, 3:1 control borders, ARIA corrections, palette hygiene and dead-code removal. Snapshot: `.impeccable/critique/`.
12. **Acceptance cycle 2 (session 2)** — second dual-agent critique. New states and behaviours: an infeasible mission now explains itself (violation tally and excluded hulls from the engine) in the panel, Results, Analytics and Routes; the mission panel describes the plan being sailed after a re-plan (carried "to arrival" totals, active-plan facts, original selection demoted); multi-select stops and segmented options use off-white so yellow stays on the selected plan, the primary action and the active rail item; restricted zones and their labels are grey until the active plan actually enters one; Space/Escape act only while Command is visible; a WebGL context loss shows a "Restore map" state; a screen-reader live region announces re-plans and arrival only; the "Safest" plan label is awarded only when it lowers tail risk by at least 0.02 against the Balanced plan, so a table of equal CVaR values no longer carries an unearned label. Critique trend: 26/40 → 28/40 (snapshots in `.impeccable/critique/`).
13. **Visual world, third redesign ("Signal Yellow") and globe removal** (5 September 2026) — on the user's direction (yellow main, black secondary, no globe, a smooth semi-3D main frame, smooth animations, "non-slop") the world was replaced again. The WebGL globe was removed: Command now renders the operating region as a projected SVG chart (Mercator-shaped y, linear x) presented as a tilted 2.5D plate with pointer parallax, wheel zoom, drag pan and keyboard control; marks, glyphs and type are counter-scaled so they keep constant pixel size at any zoom or viewport. Motion (`motion/react`) owns the plate entrance, the route draw-in, the superseded-route fade, panel presence and the parallax springs; the vessel moves on the animation frame; everything honours reduced motion. The mission panel and vessel dossier became yellow columns beside the plate (bottom sheets under 1100px) instead of overlays. Typography moved to Geist and Geist Mono (bundled), icons to Phosphor (no hand-drawn SVG icons), all corners to 2px. Copy was audited: no em- or en-dashes and no middle-dot separators in user-visible strings; ghost values are drawn as "···". The three.js dependency remains only for the procedural vessel model. Engine, scenarios, interactivity and provenance labels unchanged; typecheck, lint, 42 tests and the production build green.
14. **Flat 2D chart** (6 September 2026). The tilted 2.5D plate from decision 13 was replaced on the user's direction with a completely flat 2D chart. Why: the plate was buggy in practice. Zoom-to-cursor and drag had to invert a perspective transform (transformed-box hit math), drag panned only once zoomed, the route turned into dashes at zoom because the draw-in used a dash pattern scaled with the camera, land was nearly invisible on the black plate, the wind field read as clutter, the tilted trapezoid bled above the stage under the top-left overlays, and the entrance animation left the plate blank in some renderers. What replaced it: a flat SVG chart filling the stage (no perspective, rotate or parallax anywhere) with a proper camera (wheel and pinch zoom to the cursor from 1x to 8x, drag pan with inertia, double-click zoom, fit-to-route, follow-vessel, keyboard + - 0 arrows f v), its own small controls column at bottom-right and a scale bar at bottom-left. Two chart modes in the store (`ui.chartMode`): "chart", a light nautical chart with pale water, a soft shelf tint, sand land and a hairline coast where the plan is black and the mission vessel the one yellow mark; and "night", the black plate with the yellow plan and off-white travelled track. All chart colours come from `THEMES[mode]` (`src/components/chart/theme.ts`) through a chart context; no layer hard-codes a colour; marks and type are counter-scaled so they keep a constant pixel size at any zoom. The chart chrome was reduced to a minimalist map UI in the manner of modern boating-chart apps (their calm hierarchy, none of their branding): one row at top-left (scenario pack, Layers tab), the Mission block at top-right only while the panel is closed, the caption above the scale bar; the Layers drawer gained the mode switch and follow-vessel control and reads its legend swatches from the theme. Motion is now camera eases (300 to 700 ms), the 1.2 s route draw-in, vessel smoothing, the storm rotation and caption presence, all honouring reduced motion. Copy remains free of dashes.
15. **Random voyages** (6 September 2026). On the user's direction the fixed Mormugao to Singapore voyage was replaced by a random route across the whole chart region. Why: a single memorised route made the corridor search, the weather fields, the cyclone, the zones and every narrative look scripted; a random origin, destination, cargo, departure date and season on every page load shows that all of it is computed for the route in hand, and a seed makes any voyage reproducible for judges. How: the port network grew to about fourteen ports (src/data/ports.ts), each with an approach chain to open sea and an export list; `buildScenario(packId, voyageSeed)` (src/data/scenarios/index.ts, generator in src/engine/voyage.ts) draws the voyage with the seeded `Rng` and materialises the pack's conditions (season, storm track placed relative to the corridor, fuel menu, fleet openness) plus scenario zones, fleet positions and landmarks for that route; the store keeps `voyageSeed` and the materialised `scenario`, and every consumer reads the store (never a static scenario table). `setScenario` rebuilds the current seed under another pack; `newVoyage(seed?)` draws a fresh seed or rebuilds a given one. The seed is data text in the mission panel, Routes and Settings; New voyage sits in all three; Settings reproduces a seed. Analytics' scenario comparison rebuilds every pack for the current seed so the comparison is across conditions on one voyage. Honesty labels: only Mormugao and Singapore carry sourced facts; every new port's facts are marked scenario or derived and say so on the port page and in the panel's provenance line; zones, depths and fields remain synthetic approximations; the fixed reference voyage (seed 0) survives only for tests. Region names in captions come from the position (src/engine/regions.ts), not from a fixed longitude ladder.
16. **Visual world re-coloured ("Signal Green")** (6 September 2026). On the user's direction, from supplied design mocks, the world was re-coloured from "Signal Yellow" to a pale green theme; the brief was to keep the UI identical and change nothing but the colours. What changed: the CSS token layer in `src/styles/app.css`, where the ground `--y0` is now a pale green #f4fbec with #e8f5db and #dcefc9 as its hover and pressed tints, the body carries a soft diagonal wash from #eff8e4 through #f7fcf1 to #ffffff, the black structure was re-toned to #151614 / #1c1d1a / #24251f / #313329 with the chart plate at #101210, and the ink ramp on black became a neutral off-white (#f4f4ef, #d5d6d1, #a3a3a0, #5c5d5a) in place of the warm cream; the chart theme (`src/components/chart/theme.ts`), where light-chart water is #d3e6f2 and its land #f4f1e7, the mission vessel is green #8fd66e in both modes and the night plan is #bfe7a2; and a new JavaScript palette module, `src/app/palette.ts`, the one code-side copy of the colours for the places that cannot read a CSS custom property (Recharts props, three.js materials, canvas textures), which replaced the hard-coded hexes previously scattered through that code. The signal moved from yellow to green in three roles: `--g-fill` #bfe7a2 fills on black (the primary action on black, the active rail item, the replay playhead), `--g-ink` #cbe9ab is type on a black block (so the primary action on the pale ground, selected chips and hull rows) and `--g-bright` #8fd66e is the brightest mark (the brand shield, the mission vessel on the chart, chart accent lines). What did not change: the layout and spacing scale, the typography, the motion, every component and its markup, the chart symbology and the zone conventions, the regulatory magenta (#a23b8f on the light chart, #d26bc1 on the night plate) and the alarm red (#a8261c on the pale ground, #ef4b3f on black). The Two-Ground, Signal, Alarm, Regulatory, Ghost and One Plate rules all still hold, with green in the signal role instead of yellow. Recorded in `DESIGN.md` (frontmatter colours, north star, Colors, Named Rules), `PRODUCT.md` and `README.md`; typecheck and lint clean.
